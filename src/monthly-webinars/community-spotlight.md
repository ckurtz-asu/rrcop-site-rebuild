## UCF's Approach to CMMC Level 1: with the processes & tools that also support CMMC Level 2

August 12, 2026

Presented By: Tammie McClellan, University of Central Florida

CMMC Level 1 may only require a subset of the controls found in Level 2, but institutions still face important decisions about processes, documentation, governance, and technical implementation. In this session, the University of Central Florida shares how it approached CMMC Level 1 as a campus capability rather than a one-time compliance exercise, intentionally selecting processes and tools that also support future CMMC Level 2 requirements. Whether your institution is preparing for its first Level 1 assessment or thinking longer-term, this session offers practical ideas for building once and maturing over time.

Poll Question: How is your institution approaching CMMC Level 1 / Federal Contract Information (FCI)? [see community results](https://www.regulatedresearch.org/resources/polls#h.25q8wevw7li6)

## Dynamic Documentation: Wiki, Document as Code, and Scripted SSPs

May 13, 2026 | Institutional Showcase

Presenters:

- Erik Deumens, University of Florida
- Irene Kopaliani, Princeton University
- Tommy Tunks, University of Michigan
- John Rineck, University of Wisconsin-Madison

Maintaining accurate, audit-ready System Security Plans (SSPs) and compliance documentation is one of the most persistent challenges in regulated research. Static documents quickly become outdated, inconsistent, and difficult to scale. In this panel-style webinar, three practitioners from leading research institutions share how they have broken free from "framework rigidity" by adopting dynamic documentation strategies — from wiki-based approaches and document-as-code methodologies to scripting-driven SSP generation.

## Do you know your campus government relations team?

November 2024 | Community Spotlight

Presented by: Jay Gallman | Jeremy Hopkins | Melissa Vetterkind | Katie Lipe of Duke University

The flow of information on campuses can be quite complex, making it difficult to be sure that all the need-to-know parties are aware of things that impact their work. For over a year Duke has had formalized monthly meetings with their state and federal government relations team. This has allowed the IT Security Office and Government Relations to share concerns and provide information about coming regulatory changes. In this session the Government Relations people speak about their roles on campus, the IT Security staff members speak about their roles, and then they discuss what they bring to the table when they meet.
