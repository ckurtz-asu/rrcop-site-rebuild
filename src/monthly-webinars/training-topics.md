## Strengthening Research Security: Introducing NSF's SECURE Center & SECURE Analytics

June 10, 2026 | Training Topic

Presented By: Dr. Lisa Nichols, SECURE Center Deputy Director and Dr. Ivett Leyva, SECURE Analytics Co-PI

This session provides an overview of the National Science Foundation's SECURE Center and SECURE Analytics initiatives, highlighting their role in advancing research security, risk awareness, and data-informed decision-making across the research ecosystem. Attendees will gain insight into available tools, resources, and collaborative opportunities designed to support institutions in identifying and mitigating security risks while maintaining an open and innovative research environment.

- NSF SECURE Center — built for academic institutions, non-profit research centers, and small and medium businesses to meet the challenge of securing US innovation while maintaining a vibrant and open research ecosystem. [secure-center.org](https://www.secure-center.org/)
- NSF SECURE Analytics — engages in four core areas of work, all focused on helping universities, research organizations, businesses, and national laboratories make more informed decisions about research security: (1) Landscape Analysis, (2) Risk Modeling, (3) Tools & Technology, (4) Training & Resources. [secure-analytics.org](https://secure-analytics.org/)

## Enhancing Institutional Cybersecurity Through 14 Controls

March 11, 2026 | Training Topic

Presented By: Michael Corn, Vantage Technology Consulting Group

Institutions are encouraged to think carefully about cybersecurity. This is driven by the ever-growing number of cyber incidents, emerging requirements like NSPM-33, internal assessments within institutions, and external requirements from funding agencies. Toward this end, it is useful to look at what NSF has added for its larger projects. In September, NSF updated its research infrastructure guide to include 14 specific controls. Mike will provide an overview of what they are, how they're intended to be understood, and discuss the implications of each for institutions and security practitioners. In short Mike argues — pay attention, but don't panic.

## Sustainable Documentation: Debrief of CPPC'25 Full Day Workshop

Training Topic | July 13, 2025

Presented By: Louis Daher, Will Drake, Ryan Duitman, Carolyn Ellis, Wendy Epley, Laura Raderman

At the CPPC 2025 Workshop ([Avoiding Shelfware: Building Sustainable Research Compliance Documentation](https://events.educause.edu/cybersecurity-and-privacy-professionals-conference/2025/agenda/avoiding-lsquoshelfwarersquo-building-sustainable-research-compliance-documentationnbspseparate-regi)), ~50 community peers of research security and compliance professionals gathered for a full-day, in-person session focused on improving the sustainability and coordination of research documentation efforts. Five working groups each focused on a distinct, persistent challenge, developing shared language, peer strategies, and practical recommendations for the community.

This webinar provides a summary of insights and proposed solutions emerging from the day and these selected common challenge areas:

1. Compliance is not integrated into daily work
2. "Not my job" — role confusion and resource gaps
3. Cultural barriers to sustainable documentation
4. Compliance comes last
5. Program-level consistency and accountability

## Flipping the Script on CMMC with AFRL Digital Research

Training Topic | March 2025

Presented By: Dan Berrigan, Lead, AFRL Worldwide Research Collaboration | Digital Capabilities Directorate | Air Force Research Laboratory

The Air Force Research Laboratory (AFRL) consistently struggles with collaborating in an increasingly diverse scientific landscape. Security and compliance drive restrictions that result in mailing hard drives of data across the world. In response, AFRL has developed a digital ecosystem built to foster collaboration, innovation, and research amongst its government, academic, and industry partners. This presentation provides an overview of AFRL's digital ecosystem, which includes Google Workspace for productivity tools, the AFRL Digital Laboratory Environment for commercial engineering tools, and Google Cloud Platform (GCP) as a research and development cloud. This talk also discusses how AFRL's digital ecosystem helps its collaborators meet compliance requirements while providing access to the latest commercial cloud technologies and services.

## C3PAO Perspective on Sponsorship and Governance

Training Topic | January 2025

Facilitated by: Barb Schnell, University of Colorado, Boulder, Associate Director Secure Research Computing

Panel:

- Thomas Graham, Ph.D., VP and CISO at Redspin, a division of Clearwater, CCA, CCI
- Fernando Machado, CISO Cybersec Investments, CCA, CCP
- Mike Snyder, Director of Training and Credentialing, The Cyber AB, CISM, CCA, CPI
- Mathew Titcombe, CEO and founder at Peak InfoSec, CCA

Effective sponsorship and governance for compliance-related policies, Organizational Defined Parameters (ODPs) and organizational roles and responsibilities can be challenging in a highly distributed and consensus-oriented culture such as that found in higher education institutions. Yet it is a requirement for a solid research cybersecurity program and an enabling factor for certifications. This panel discussion engages this topic from the perspective of CMMC 3rd Party Assessment Organizations (C3PAOs) in terms of what an auditor may consider in their assessment and the impact of an organization's governance and related processes on achieving certification.

## Preparing and Engaging in Third-Party Assessors

Presented By: Erik Deumens, University of Florida; Damon Armour, North Carolina State University

August 10, 2022 | Training Topic

Erik Deumens, University of Florida, describes the general flow of the external assessment process by a company or by an Office of Internal Audit. UF has some experience because it has gone through a few of them. This will be useful for many institutions as more compliance requirements will include assessment by a third party.

Damon Armour, North Carolina State University, presented on recent third-party assessments by consulting firms on establishing a more formal IT risk management approach, meeting NIST 800-171 requirements for both DFARS and GLBA compliant environments and maintaining HIPAA Security Rule compliance for a non-academic medical institution. Each assessment resulted in commonalities that were foundational to having effective compliance, cybersecurity and risk management programs.
